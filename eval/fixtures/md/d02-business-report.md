# Northwind Components — Annual Operations Report 2025

Prepared by the Finance and Operations Office, March 2026.

## Executive Summary

Fiscal year 2025 was the strongest year in Northwind Components’ history. Revenue grew by 14.2 % to 48.6 million euros, driven mainly by the industrial sensor line and by new distribution agreements in Central Europe. Gross margin improved by 1.8 percentage points as the second production line in Wrocław reached full utilisation and scrap rates fell below 2 % for the first time. Operating expenses rose more slowly than revenue, which lifted the operating margin to 11.9 %.

The year also brought challenges. Component shortages in the first quarter delayed roughly 6 % of planned shipments, and freight costs remained above pre-2020 levels throughout the year. Customer concentration increased slightly: the five largest customers now account for 41 % of revenue, compared with 38 % a year earlier. Management considers this level acceptable but has set a target to bring it below 35 % by 2028.[^1]

This report summarises the key performance indicators, the main findings of the annual operations review, and the recommendations that the board approved in its February meeting. Detailed financial statements are published separately in the audited annual accounts.

## Findings

The operations review identified the following findings:

- **Capacity.** The second production line operated at 87 % of nominal capacity on average, up from 64 % in 2024. The first line remains the bottleneck for the legacy product range.
- **Quality.** Scrap rates fell from 2.9 % to 1.8 %. Customer returns declined by a third, and no product recall was required.
- **Supply chain.** Lead times for microcontrollers averaged 21 weeks in the first quarter and 12 weeks by year end. Dual sourcing now covers 70 % of critical components.
- **Energy.** Electricity costs fell by 9 % after the installation of rooftop photovoltaic panels, which supplied about 18 % of total consumption.[^2]
- **People.** Staff turnover was 7.4 %, below the industry average of 11 %. Training hours per employee increased to 32.
- **Digital sales.** Orders placed through the online portal grew to 23 % of all orders, and the average order processing time fell from 3.1 days to 1.6 days.

> Note: The capacity figures exclude the four-week maintenance shutdown in August, which was planned and agreed with customers in advance.

## Recommendations

The board approved the following recommendations for 2026:

1. Invest 2.4 million euros in a third production line dedicated to the industrial sensor range, with commissioning planned for the fourth quarter.
2. Extend dual sourcing to all critical components by the end of the year, starting with power semiconductors and connectors.
3. Expand the distribution network into the Nordic countries through two new partner agreements.
4. Increase the share of renewable electricity to 30 % through a long-term power purchase agreement.
5. Launch a customer diversification programme with a dedicated key-account team for mid-sized customers.
6. Replace the legacy warehouse management system, which no longer receives security updates.
7. Publish the first sustainability report according to the European reporting standards.[^3]

## Segment Review

**Industrial sensors.** The industrial sensor line remained the main growth driver, with revenue up 22 % to 21.3 million euros. Demand came mainly from machine builders modernising their equipment with condition monitoring. The new temperature and vibration sensor family launched in April accounted for 3.9 million euros in its first eight months, well above the plan of 2.5 million euros. Average selling prices held steady despite competitive pressure from Asian suppliers, because customers valued the longer warranty and the local technical support.

**Automotive components.** Revenue from automotive components grew by 6 % to 15.8 million euros. Volumes were affected by production cuts at two customers in the first half of the year, but the second half recovered strongly. The company won a nomination for a new seat-position sensor that will enter series production in 2027, with expected lifetime revenue of about 18 million euros. Margins in this segment remain lower than in industrial sensors because of the strict price-down clauses typical of automotive supply contracts.

**Services and spare parts.** Services and spare parts contributed 11.5 million euros, an increase of 11 %. Calibration services grew fastest, supported by new accredited laboratory capacity in Poznań. Service contracts now cover more than 4,000 installed systems, providing a stable recurring revenue base that is less sensitive to the economic cycle than new equipment sales.

## Outlook

The order backlog at the end of the year stood at 14.8 million euros, about 30 % higher than a year earlier. Assuming no major disruption in the supply of components, management expects revenue growth of 8–10 % in 2026 and a stable operating margin. Capital expenditure will rise to around 4.1 million euros, mainly for the third production line and the warehouse system.

Risks to this outlook include renewed component shortages, a slowdown in the European automotive sector, and further increases in energy prices. The company monitors these risks monthly and reports them to the audit committee each quarter.
