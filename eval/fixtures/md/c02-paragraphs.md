Telecommunications networks are designed with redundancy in mind, because a single failed link can isolate an entire region. Engineers therefore plan at least two independent paths between any pair of major nodes, and they monitor latency continuously. When the round-trip time on a path exceeds 40 ms for more than ten seconds, traffic is rerouted automatically. The procedure is simple in principle but difficult in practice, since rerouting itself causes short bursts of congestion. Over the last decade, operators have reduced the average recovery time from about 50 s to under 2 s, largely through better software rather than through additional hardware.

Writing documentation is a craft that rewards patience. A good manual anticipates the reader’s questions, answers them in the order they arise, and avoids the temptation to explain everything at once. Short sentences help, but so does structure: headings that describe tasks, lists that describe steps, and tables that collect values a reader will want to compare.
