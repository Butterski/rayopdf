# RayoPDF 1.1.0 on the harness, 2026-11-26

1.1.0 is [1.0.0](v1.0.0-2026-11-25.md)'s converter made faster as a fresh process, with the OCR
add-on reduced to the engine it ships, and with two small models and a WebAssembly SIMD build
that were tried and refused. Every speed step was output-identical (`bench.py identical` against
the step before over 5,886 documents in ten option sets, 58,860 comparisons a step, 0 differ), and
the harness gives 1.0.0's quality numbers again, every output equal but for the version JSON
names. Quality was measured on 2026-11-25 on `e1d9f98`, whose converter is the release's (the
commits after it regenerate `src/hot.ld`, output byte-identical, and take the WebAssembly module
back to its scalar build), the holdouts (`readoc` and the weasyprint producer) once and aggregate
only; speed on CPU 3 against the 1.0.0 release binary. Same machine and harness as
[the baselines](baselines-2026-11-05.md); the release check is
[release-check-2026-11-26](release-check-2026-11-26.md).

## New since 1.0.0

- **The fresh process:** what a process did once is now built in (the base encodings' texts and
  the BMP's UTF-8 table are tables made at build time), a one-shot conversion (`--convert`) frees
  each stage's storage for the stages after it, an inflated stream of 1 MiB or more goes to a
  huge-page mapping, body-size votes and running keys need no hash maps, the content lexer takes
  the space after an operator in the same step, and JSON and report numbers are written by
  `std::to_chars` ([performance.md](../development/performance.md#twenty-fifth-round-the-fresh-process-2026-11-25)).
  A one-page conversion takes 4 % to 5 % less time and 20 fewer page faults; the heaviest odl
  page, a vector drawing, 18 % less time and a quarter of its faults.
- **OCR add-on:** the ncnn engine only. The ONNX Runtime and Tesseract backends and the medium
  tier, none of which ever shipped, are gone with their options (`--engine`, `--tesseract`,
  `--lang`, `--tier medium`: usage error); the add-on's image is `rayopdf-ocr:2`, its models are
  1.0.0's byte for byte, and it builds in 6 minutes instead of about 50.
- **Tools:** `bench.py fresh --per-doc` keeps each document's figures; `bench.py wasm` times the
  WebAssembly module and compares its output with the native program's.
- **Removed:** the table-gap prototype (`--features table-gaps` is a usage error) and product
  functions only the tests called.
- **Python:** the PyPI description's images and links point at the release's files.

1.0.0 measured 0.912 ms fresh and 2,207 pages/s on 2026-11-24; the same binary gave 0.924 ms and
2,203 here: the comparison is the interleaved one. 1.1.0 runs fewer instructions in every group
(`--batch --workers=1`): odl −0.94 %, heavy10 −1.17 %, the synthetic corpus's six multi-page

## WebAssembly and footprint

The npm package's module under Node.js 22 (`bench.py wasm`, CPU 3, 2026-11-25), 1.0.0's against
1.1.0's over odl in three alternating runs: 1,287 → 1,260, 1,258 → 1,252 and 1,221 → 1,391 pages/s,
medians of 0.59 to 0.61 ms a page in both; the sums follow odl 141, whose time in one module
ranges from 18 to 54 ms depending on when V8 optimises its code. The native program takes a
median 0.37 ms a page in a running process. The module's output is the native program's on all
200 odl pages and on 5,867 of the 5,886 documents of the speed steps' inputs, images off; the
other 19 differed before 1.1 too ([release check](release-check-2026-11-26.md#output-identity)).

Footprint (the Release workflow's dry run of the release's code): the static `rayopdf` 4,343,736 B
(1.0.0: 3,991,968 B, +8.8 %, the tables now built in and `std::to_chars`' for JSON numbers), the
Windows `rayopdf.exe` 5,587,414 B (5,190,054 B), the Linux archive 2,190,176 B (2,011,721 B), the
npm package 1,334,149 B (1,156,850 B); every package and peak RSS per document are in the
[release check](release-check-2026-11-26.md#e-footprint).

## Tried and not landed

Both quality steps went through the strict gate (no gated metric more than 0.0001 below its best
on any development or validation suite); each failed, took its one fallback, failed again and was
dropped. Both models were trained on all 69,375 pages of DocLayNet's training split
([ocr_ml_decision.md](../development/ocr_ml_decision.md)):

- **Edge rows v4:** the furniture model retrained on every training page and only on the rows the
  engine lets it decide, with two features more; doclaynet-dev furniture F1 of rows 0.874 → 0.894
  as v3, but olmOCR `headers_footers` −0.0040; with v3's 23 features, `headers_footers` −0.0013
  and the synthetic corpus's untagged MHS −0.0068. v3 stays.
- **Table runs:** a model for the borderless runs the table rules refuse; DocLayNet `table_f1`
  +0.027 (development) and +0.019 (validation), olmOCR `table_tests` +0.005, but odl TEDS −0.0028
  and READoc's arXiv table scores down; at precision 0.95 still odl TEDS −0.0028 and
  OmniParseBench `text` −0.0003 and −0.0007. The rules stay.

Of the speed steps, the WebAssembly module built with SIMD was faster on most pages but slower on
the heaviest, and its pages a second over odl moved −5.6 % to +3.9 % against the scalar build,
short of the 5 % asked: the module stays scalar. It and the other speed steps tried and rejected
are in [performance.md](../development/performance.md#tried-and-rejected-2026-11-25).
